# How the cello is modelled

What the engine does and where each number comes from. `docs/PHASE0.md` has the experiments the
design rests on; `docs/PLAN.md` what comes next.

## Overview: a note

1. **Velocity** sets the dynamic, on a continuous axis from p (0) to f (3), as the library's sfz maps
   its layers: each layer's middle velocity (16, 48, 80, 112) plays that layer exactly.
2. **The onset**: the first 0.4 s of the nearest measured note's down-bow recording at the nearest
   dynamic, transposed to the note's pitch (at most 1.5 semitones) and read at the host's rate. It is
   the bow's catch, its scratch and its pitch glide as recorded (Phase 0: keeping the glide brings
   the attack from 2.9 to 1.6 % of the recording).
3. **The hand-off**, over 0.30-0.40 s of the recording's time: a raised-cosine crossfade from the
   recording to the model. Each of the model's harmonics starts at the recording's level and phase at
   0.35 s, so the two are in step and the crossfade adds nothing of its own.
4. **The model**: a bank of harmonics (up to 160, to 16 kHz) at the note's pitch, gliding over
   0.15 s from the onset's levels to the steady spectrum: **source × body**, plus the measured notes'
   own corrections nearby. With **bow noise** in octave bands under them, **pulsing** once per period
   of the string as the recordings' does.
5. **The bow's movement** comes in over 0.3 s once the hand-off is over: the pitch wanders, the level
   moves, and each harmonic moves on its own, all as measured from the recordings. The body follows
   the pitch, so as it moves (or a vibrato swings it) each harmonic's level follows its body response.
6. **The release**: the bow leaves. The movement stops; each harmonic decays at its octave band's rate
   measured from the staccatos; the noise stops in 15 ms.

Output is the recordings' level −6 dB (`kTrimDb`): four strings at f peak near −7 dBFS.

The voice keeps its own clock: everything that isn't per sample (the movement, the glide, the release,
the spectrum following the pitch) is stepped every 32 samples of the note's own time (`kGrid`), never
per render call, so the sound doesn't depend on the host's block size (the kit checks blocks of 1,
37, 512 and 4096 samples: identical).

## The data (`src/CelloData.h`)

Generated by `tools/make_header.py` from `tools/cello.json` and `tools/cello_onsets.npz`, which
`tools/analyze.py` measures from the Karoryfer × bigcat cello (CC0), and two calibrations
(`tools/life_adjust.json`, `tools/noise_adjust.json`, below). Never edit the header by hand.

```
git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/sfzinstruments/karoryfer-bigcat.cello ../bigcat
python3 -I tools/analyze.py ../bigcat tools/cello.json tools/cello_onsets.npz    # ~5 min
python3 -I tools/make_header.py tools/cello.json tools/cello_onsets.npz src/CelloData.h
# build; then, only if the analysis or the engine's noise or movement changed:
python3 -I tools/calibrate_noise.py build/strings_render tools/cello.json tools/noise_adjust.json  # ×3,
#   make_header.py and a build after each (~2 min a pass)
python3 -I tools/life.py ../bigcat --render build/strings_render --json life.json              # ~3 min
python3 -I tools/calibrate_life.py life.json tools/life_adjust.json                           # ×2-3,
#   make_header.py, a build and life.py after each
```

Re-analysing keeps both calibrations (they live in their own files); delete a file to start that
calibration over.

Per measured note (17, C2-C6 a minor third apart) and dynamic (p, mp, mf, f), from the down-bow
sustain:

| | |
|---|---|
| `onset` | the first 0.4 s, 16-bit at 44.1 kHz (68 × 17640 samples: 2.4 MB) |
| `handF0` | the pitch at the hand-off (0.35 s), which the onset is transposed from |
| `handDb`, `handPh` | each harmonic's level and phase at 0.35 s (heterodyned, `tools/harmonics.py`) |
| `level` | the steady harmonic level (rms of the harmonic part, 0.4 s to the end) |
| `corr` | the note's own steady spectrum against source × body, per harmonic |
| `noiseDb` | the bow noise: each octave band filter's gain (below) |
| `grainDepth`, `grainPhase` | the noise's pulse per band: how deep, and where in the period (below) |

Per measured note: `releaseDbS[band]`, the decay after the bow stops, from the staccatos; and
`lifePitch`, `lifeLevel`, `lifeHarm[octave]`, the bow's movement (below). For the whole instrument,
`source[dynamic][harmonic]` and `body` (dB on knots every 1/24 octave, 55 Hz-16 kHz): the source ×
body fit (`tools/bodyfit.py`) over both bows of every sustain; and `lifeK[harmonic]`.

### Source × body

Each sustain's steady harmonic levels are fitted as one level per file, plus the bowed string's
spectrum by harmonic number per dynamic (`source`), plus one body response by frequency for the
whole cello (`body`). Leave one note out, it predicts a note it hasn't seen to 4.1 dB per harmonic
(median), against 7.6 dB for transposing the nearest note, and the same note's other bow at 3.1 dB:
nearer than transposing in 136 of 136 files. The body needs its detail: 1/6-octave knots gave 5.1 dB.

**Between the measured notes** the steady spectrum is source × body at the note's own harmonics
(dynamics interpolated). **At and near a measured note** its own correction is added, read along
frequency (so the body's peaks stay where they are) and fading out over 3 semitones (`kCorrSpan`),
so a measured note sounds as recorded and the step to its neighbour is gradual. Adding the
neighbours' corrections to an unseen note didn't help it (4.05 → 3.97 dB), so between them the model
is source × body alone. The level is interpolated in dB between the measured notes and dynamics.

The body and corrections belong to frequency, so they're recomputed as the pitch moves (past 3
cents: their detail is 50 cents wide), and the Body control scales them (0: the source alone).

Harmonics go to 16 kHz (`kFMax`; 160 at most, which C2-D#3 reach first). At 12 kHz, as at first, the
recordings' harmonics above it were measured as noise and played as hiss: the top band between the
harmonics was 8 dB too loud (12-15 dB on mid and high notes).

### The bow's movement (`tools/life.py`)

A held bowed note isn't still. Over each sustain's steady part `tools/life.py` measures:

| | recordings (median) | renders |
|---|---|---|
| pitch, trend removed | 1.58 cents rms, spread fairly evenly over 0.25-16 Hz | 1.37 |
| level | 1.44 dB, mostly slower than 2 Hz | 1.22 |
| each harmonic against the level | 2.38 dB, nearly independent (correlation between them 0.03) | 2.30 (0.04) |
| ... by harmonic number | 1.3 dB on the fundamental, 1.7 the 2nd, 2.4 the 8th, 3.0 the 20th | 1.0, 1.3, 2.4, 2.5 |
| the noise's pulse at the period, 250 Hz-16 kHz | 0.03 0.07 0.11 0.17 0.22 0.37 0.51 | 0.08 0.15 0.15 0.15 0.14 0.31 0.43 |

(Spectra per octave of modulation frequency compare the same way: `life.py` prints them.) The
movement varies by dynamic with no clear trend (pitch: mp 0.96 c, f 2.31), so the engine stores it
per note, pooled over the 4 dynamics × 2 bows and smoothed across notes (a median of three); the
harmonics' rise with harmonic number is one law for the instrument (`lifeK`: a line in log2 k fitted
to k ≤ 20, held from 1.25× the 20th's above: a judgment).

The engine plays each as band-limited noise, one RBJ band-pass per octave (0.25-32 Hz, Q √2) scaled
to its measured rms, on the voice's 32-sample step:

- **Pitch** (cents) and **level** (dB, shared with the noise) as sums of octave bands.
- **Each harmonic's own movement**: three two-octave bands (0.25-16 Hz), closed above 20 Hz by a
  second-order low-pass, updated every 4th step (345 Hz at 44.1 kHz). It is measured against the
  note's level, so it moves energy between the harmonics and leaves the level alone: each step takes
  out what it would add in total.
- Each harmonic's rotator carries its amplitude, and each step aims it at its new level by a growth
  per sample: a smooth ramp at no cost per sample.
- The filters start in their steady state (a draw of their state's covariance), so the slowest
  movement (1-2 s to build up from rest) is there from the first moment.

Lessons:

- **Calibrate it** (`tools/calibrate_life.py`, the piano's calibrate_noise.py's way): `life.py`'s
  slowest octaves read low (a 3 s stretch, trend removed, a 2.6 s window). The data is built from
  those readings and the renders are read the same way, so the bias counts twice: they measured at
  half. Three passes of a per-octave correction brought them level (`tools/life_adjust.json`).
- **A harmonic's movement needs a steep top.** A band-pass's 6 dB/octave skirt left sidebands 20-30 dB
  under each harmonic, 30-130 Hz from it: over the bow noise between the harmonics, hiding its pulse
  (0.04 measured against 0.2-0.4) and roughening the tone. So does a gain changed in steps (32
  samples), which is why the gains ramp.
- **Not by the distance.** A random path can't follow the recording's own, so the perceptual
  distance can't judge the movement: two renders of the same note (two random paths) are 2.8 % apart
  (median, 1.7-4.9), and the distance rose from 3.0 to 4.3 % with the movement on while its signed
  differences (which way the sound is wrong) stayed within 1 %. The statistics are the measure.

### The bow noise

The residual (the recording less its harmonics) over the steady part, as power in nine octave bands
(62.5 Hz-16 kHz), relative to the harmonic level. The engine plays unit-variance white noise through
two RBJ band-pass stages per band (Q 1.2), **each band from its own noise**: from one, neighbouring
bands' outputs are correlated and partly cancel where they overlap (they came out 6-13 dB short).
Their powers then add, and `make_header.py` solves the bands' gains against the filters' responses.
The noise comes in with the model during the hand-off (the onset's own noise is in the recording) and
follows the dynamic and the level's movement.

**Calibrated** (`tools/calibrate_noise.py`, as the piano's): the analysis reads the noise as the
residual, and its harmonics (heterodyned over 3 periods) take in the noise within about a third of
the harmonic spacing: on low notes, most of it. So the stored levels read low (7-8 dB at C2, nothing
from F#5 up) and the engine, playing them faithfully, measured low by that again. Three passes of a
correction per cell and band (`tools/noise_adjust.json`) bring the renders' residual to the
recordings' within 0.5 dB (median), and, checked another way (the spectrum between the harmonics,
±f0/4 notched, 34 cells), within ±1.2 dB per band (median).

**The pulse** (`grainDepth`, `grainPhase`). In the recordings the bow noise comes in bursts, once per
vibration of the string, as the bow catches and slips: its envelope's component at the fundamental's
phase, per band, is 0.2-0.5 of its mean above 2 kHz (by chance: 0.01). Where in the period it peaks is
a property of the bow's direction: down-bow and up-bow are 171° apart (median), as reversing the bow
mirrors the string's motion. The engine shapes each band's noise by u^p (u = (1 + cos(θ − ψ)) / 2,
θ the fundamental's phase from its rotator), with p for the measured depth (`grainExp`) and the
down-bow's ψ (the onsets are down-bow), normalised to keep the band's power. Grain scales the depth.

### The release

The sustains are cut mid-bow for looping, so the only release in the library is the staccatos', which
end in the string's own decay. Per staccato, each harmonic's level is fitted as a line from where the
note has fallen 6 dB from its peak to 36 dB; harmonics are pooled by octave band, then over every
dynamic and round robin of the note (15-16 staccatos), then smoothed across neighbouring notes (a
median of three per band, in log: one note's set scatters, C5's fundamental 163 dB/s against A4's 31).
Result: 30-60 dB/s low in the range, rising to 150-200 dB/s at the top and in the highest bands.

A key let go during the onset: the recording fades at 80 dB/s (`kOnsetReleaseDbS`, a judgment) and
still hands over, the harmonics starting at the level it has decayed to, so a short note rings down
as a string does.

## Controls

All are modulation destinations; none is measured except at its default (which is the cello as
recorded). Ranges and sizes are judgments.

| Control | ID | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Life | `life.amount` | 100 % (0-300) | all of the movement together; 0 is Phase 1's still note |
| Pitch Wander | `life.pitch` | 100 % (0-400) | the pitch's share |
| Flicker | `life.flicker` | 100 % (0-400) | each harmonic's own movement |
| Speed | `life.speed` | 100 % (25-400) | the movement's time scale (the filters retuned; their rms kept) |
| Body | `tone.body` | 100 % (0-200) | the body response and corrections, scaled; the level kept |
| Bow Noise | `tone.noise` | 0 dB (−30…+24) | the noise's level; it sits 35-45 dB under the tone, so a few dB is subtle |
| Grain | `tone.grain` | 100 % (0-300) | the noise's pulse depth |
| Release | `play.release` | 100 % (25-400) | the release's time scale |

Life and Flicker multiply, so a harmonic's own movement is held to −40…+12 dB, and the level's to
±12 dB. Presets: *Still Bow*, *Restless Bow*, *Bare String* (Body 0), *Big Body*, *Airy Bow*, *Smooth
Bow*, *Long Ring*, *Shimmer*, *Vibrato* (LFO 1 into Pitch: 5.5 Hz, ±20 cents, fading in over 0.6 s).

How far each is from Init by the perceptual distance (C3, C4; Init against itself, another random
path: 1.9, 3.3): Bare String 32, 20; Big Body 10, 5.4; Shimmer 4.8, 6.0; Restless Bow 2.0, 3.3;
Still Bow 1.3, 2.3; Vibrato 0.8, 0.6; Airy Bow 0.4, 0.9; Smooth Bow 0.0, 0.1. The distance can't tell
noise from tone within a band, nor judge movement (above), so the noise and movement presets are
bigger to the ear than these numbers; Bare String and Big Body are the timbre changes.

## How close it is (`tools/measure.py`)

Every measured note and dynamic rendered as recorded (the layer's middle velocity, held, 44.1 kHz),
against its down-bow recording over 0-3 s by the perceptual distance (`tools/perceptual.py`, the
piano's): median **4.3 %** (attack 0, early 1.8, sustain 10.3). The attack is the recording itself,
so 0 there is by construction.

- **The timbre alone** (the recording's level movement applied to the render after the hand-off):
  **3.3 %** (sustain 7.6). Both were 3.0 and 2.3 % with the note held still (Phase 1): the rise is the
  random path (above), not a bias.
- **The yardstick**: the same note's up-bow recording against the down-bow, 20.1 %: another
  performance (its own envelope and onset).
- **Even along the keyboard** (every semitone C2-C6 at mf): the largest level step between
  neighbouring semitones is 3.0 dB (median 0.8), against 7.7 dB between measured notes a minor
  third apart (median 2.3).
- **The hand-off** (`tools/handoff.py`): the level 0.27-0.44 s dips 2.3 dB below the level around
  it (median), where the recordings themselves dip 1.9 dB at the same moment (the bow's own
  movement): the render dips 0.4 dB more than its own recording (median), 3.3 dB at worst. The
  movement starts after the crossfade; starting during it made the hand-off worse (0.6 dB).

## Speed

In the browser (Chromium; `synthkit/web/test/bench-web.cjs`): four low notes at f (C2, G2, D3, A3:
the most harmonics) 3.6× realtime (Phase 1: 6.8×), the test chord 6.2×; native (Release) 12×. Low
notes cost most (160 harmonics); about 60 % of the time is the harmonic bank's rotators, the rest the
movement's step and the noise (nine bands, each its own random stream). Lessons: the browser's expf/logf are
software routines, so per-harmonic gains use a polynomial `fastExp`/`fastLog2`; a normal draw costs a
log, a cosine and a root, so the movement's noise is uniform (its narrow filters make it Gaussian);
WebAssembly SIMD made no difference (the harmonic sum doesn't vectorise without fast-math), nor
did four partial sums (slower). The
workspace's default build isn't optimised (synthkit forces Release only as the top-level project):
time with `-DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release`.

## What isn't modelled (yet)

- Any bow control: pressure and position, continuous dynamics (velocity picks the dynamic at the
  start), legato, pizzicato (Phase 3). Vibrato is the kit's LFO into Pitch: the harmonics and their
  body levels follow it, but the onset doesn't.
- The movement per dynamic (pooled), and its course through a long note (one steady process).
- The up-bow recordings (only the down-bow onsets are kept: alternating bows is a later choice).
- Below C2 and above C6 the nearest note's onset is transposed further; nothing stops playing there.
