# How the cello is modelled

What the engine does and where each number comes from. `docs/PHASE0.md` has the experiments the
design rests on; `docs/PLAN.md` what comes next.

## Overview: a note

1. **Velocity** sets the dynamic, on a continuous axis from p (0) to f (3), as the library's sfz maps
   its layers: each layer's middle velocity (16, 48, 80, 112) plays that layer exactly. With
   **Dynamics From: Controller** the velocity sets only the onset, and the Dynamics control (the mod
   wheel or expression pedal) sets the sustain and moves it during the note (below: Playing).
2. **The onset**: the first 0.4 s of the nearest measured note's down-bow recording at the nearest
   dynamic (or, where that take's bow bounced, the same note's at the nearest steady dynamic:
   `tools/onset_choice.py`), transposed to the note's pitch (at most 1.5 semitones) and read at the
   host's rate. It is
   the bow's catch, its scratch and its pitch glide as recorded (Phase 0: keeping the glide brings
   the attack from 2.9 to 1.6 % of the recording).
3. **The hand-off**, over 0.30-0.40 s of the recording's time: a raised-cosine crossfade from the
   recording to the model. Each of the model's harmonics starts at the recording's level and phase at
   0.35 s, so the two are in step and the crossfade adds nothing of its own.
4. **The model**: a bank of harmonics (up to 160, to 16 kHz) at the note's pitch, gliding over
   0.15 s from the onset's levels to the steady spectrum: **source × body**, plus the measured notes'
   own corrections nearby. With **bow noise** in octave bands under them, **pulsing** once per period
   of the string as the recordings' does.
5. **The bow's movement** comes in over 0.3 s once the hand-off is over: the pitch wanders, the level
   moves, and each harmonic moves on its own, all as measured from the recordings. The body follows
   the pitch, its fine detail too (narrow resonances, read every step), so as the pitch moves (the
   wander, a vibrato, a slur) each harmonic's level follows its body response.
6. **The release**: the bow leaves. The movement stops; each harmonic decays at its octave band's rate
   measured from the staccatos; the noise stops in 15 ms.

Output is the recordings' level −6 dB (`kTrimDb`): four strings at f peak near −7 dBFS.

The voice keeps its own clock: everything that isn't per sample (the movement, the glide, the release,
the spectrum following the pitch) is stepped every 32 samples of the note's own time (`kGrid`), never
per render call, so the sound doesn't depend on the host's block size (the kit checks blocks of 1,
37, 512 and 4096 samples: identical).

## The data (`src/CelloData.h`)

(The pizzicato's data is its own header, `src/PizzData.h`: Pizzicato, below.)

Generated by `tools/make_header.py` from `tools/cello.json` and `tools/cello_onsets.npz`, which
`tools/analyze.py` measures from the Karoryfer × bigcat cello (CC0), the body's fine detail's
statistics (`tools/finebody_stats.json`, below) and three calibrations (`tools/life_adjust.json`,
`tools/noise_adjust.json`, `tools/flicker_adjust.json`, below). Never edit the header by hand.

```
git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/sfzinstruments/karoryfer-bigcat.cello ../bigcat
python3 -I tools/analyze.py ../bigcat tools/cello.json tools/cello_onsets.npz    # ~5 min
python3 -I tools/finebody.py ../bigcat --stats tools/finebody_stats.json         # ~2 min (after analyze)
python3 -I tools/onset_choice.py ../bigcat tools/onset_choice.json              # which take each onset plays
python3 -I tools/make_header.py tools/cello.json tools/cello_onsets.npz src/CelloData.h
# build; then, only if the analysis or the engine's noise or movement changed:
python3 -I tools/calibrate_noise.py build/strings_render tools/cello.json tools/noise_adjust.json  # ×3,
#   make_header.py and a build after each (~2 min a pass)
python3 -I tools/life.py ../bigcat --render build/strings_render --json life.json              # ~3 min
python3 -I tools/calibrate_life.py life.json tools/life_adjust.json                           # ×2-3,
#   make_header.py, a build and life.py after each
python3 -I tools/calibrate_flicker.py ../bigcat build/strings_render tools/flicker_adjust.json  # ×3, last,
#   make_header.py and a build after each (~10 min a pass: four paths a cell)
```

Re-analysing keeps the calibrations (they live in their own files); delete a file to start that
calibration over.

Per measured note (17, C2-C6 a minor third apart) and dynamic (p, mp, mf, f), from the down-bow
sustain:

| | |
|---|---|
| `onset` | the first 0.4 s, 16-bit at 44.1 kHz (68 × 17640 samples: 2.4 MB) |
| `handF0` | the pitch at the hand-off (0.35 s), which the onset is transposed from |
| `handDb`, `handPh` | each harmonic's level and phase at 0.35 s (heterodyned, `tools/harmonics.py`) |
| `level` | the steady harmonic level (rms of the harmonic part, 0.4 s to the end) |
| `levelEven` | the same from a smooth curve along the keyboard (Even Notes, below) |
| `onsetDyn` | the dynamic whose take plays the cell's onset (`tools/onset_choice.py`, below) |
| `corr` | the note's own steady spectrum against source × body, per harmonic |
| `noiseDb` | the bow noise: each octave band filter's gain (below) |
| `grainDepth`, `grainPhase` | the noise's pulse per band: how deep, and where in the period (below) |

Per measured note: `releaseDbS[band]`, the decay after the bow stops, from the staccatos; and
`lifePitch`, `lifeLevel[octave]`, the bow's movement (below); per note and dynamic, `lifeShape`,
`lifeShapeOct`, `lifeResidK`, `lifeResidOct`, the harmonics' own movement. For the whole instrument,
`source[dynamic][harmonic]` and `body` (dB on knots every 1/24 octave, 55 Hz-16 kHz): the source ×
body fit (`tools/bodyfit.py`) over both bows of every sustain; and `fine`, the body's fine detail
(below), every 2.5 cents.

### Which take plays the onset (`tools/onset_choice.py`)

The down-bow takes' onsets are the steadier of the two (up-bow takes dip more in most cells), but a
few bounce: the level falls and comes back within the first 0.4 s. The owner heard it on F4-A4 at
mf as "the bow bounced a little": F#4 mf's take falls 7 dB at 0.08 and 0.22 s (and goes on wobbling
to 1 s, after the model has taken over). Per take, the deepest fall below the level reached so far over
0.05-0.40 s (10 ms windows); a down-bow take falling more than 5.5 dB plays the same note's take at the
nearest dynamic falling less than 4: A2 p (7.1 dB) plays mp's, F#4 mf (7.1) f's, F#4 mp (5.8) p's,
C6 f (6.3) mf's, each at the cell's own level. The onset's patterns of movement and hand-off go with
the take; the sustain is the cell's own.

### Source × body

Each sustain's steady harmonic levels are fitted as one level per file, plus the bowed string's
spectrum by harmonic number per dynamic (`source`), plus one body response by frequency for the
whole cello (`body`). Leave one note out, it predicts a note it hasn't seen to 4.3 dB per harmonic
(median), against 7.4 dB for transposing the nearest note, and the same note's other bow at 3.1 dB:
nearer than transposing in 136 of 136 files. The body needs its detail: 1/6-octave knots gave 5.1 dB.

Harmonics are weighted by level, and those more than 50 dB under the strongest count a little
(`WEAK`, 0.1). At first they were left out: the fit never saw how the top falls away and predicted
the weakest high harmonics 8-21 dB too loud (C2's 50th, C3's 40th-50th), a fizz whose flicker was
heard as a flutter. With them the unseen-note error rose from 4.1 to 4.3 dB, and the measured notes'
corrections now cover every harmonic, the weak ones too (within −40…+24 dB).

**Between the measured notes** the steady spectrum is source × body at the note's own harmonics
(dynamics interpolated). **At and near a measured note** its own correction is added, read along
frequency (so the body's peaks stay where they are) and fading out over 3 semitones (`kCorrSpan`),
so a measured note sounds as recorded and the step to its neighbour is gradual. Adding the
neighbours' corrections to an unseen note didn't help it (4.05 → 3.97 dB), so between them the model
is source × body alone. The level is interpolated in dB between the measured notes and dynamics.

The body and corrections belong to frequency, so they're recomputed as the pitch moves (past 3
cents: their detail is 50 cents wide), and the Body control scales them (0: the source alone).

Harmonics go to 16 kHz (`kFMax`; 160 at most, which C2-D#3 reach first). At 12 kHz, as at first, the
recordings' harmonics above it were measured as noise and played as hiss: the top band between the
harmonics was 8 dB too loud (12-15 dB on mid and high notes).

### The body's fine detail (`tools/finebody.py`)

The fitted body is smooth over 50 cents, but a cello's body has narrow resonances, and as a note's
pitch moves its harmonics slide across them. That is most of what makes a string vibrato sound like
one: each harmonic's level rises and falls with the pitch, by its own amount. With the smooth body
alone a vibrato moved the pitch and little else, and sounded synthy.

`tools/finebody.py` measures it from the pitch's own wander in the sustains (a cent or two): per
harmonic, the slope of its level (against the note's level, both low-passed to 8 Hz) on the pitch.
Each note has 8 takes (4 dynamics × 2 bows), its harmonics at the same frequencies, so the body's part
is what they share:

- **It is the body, and fine.** Two takes' slopes agree only loosely (correlation 0.18 over the
  harmonics; 10 % of the slopes' variance is common to a note's takes), but what they share is not a
  tilt (a line in log k explains 3 % of it) and averages near 0 (0.03 dB per cent): narrow resonances.
- **Its size**, the common part with each take's noise taken out, per octave band from 125 Hz:
  0.23, 0.17, 0.26, 0.24, 0.34, 0.42 dB per cent (rising with frequency). And the measured notes'
  corrections, what the fitted body leaves, are 3.3 dB rms in every band.
- **Not its shape.** Note by note the estimate is about as noisy as it is large, so the cello's own
  resonances can't be read off. The engine has a fixed curve with those statistics instead (a
  judgment): the magnitude of a smooth complex Gaussian process (many overlapping resonances: sharp
  dips, rounder peaks, dips held to 25 dB), its frequency axis stretched so its slope follows the
  measured line per band (checked on the table's own grid and corrected, three passes: within about
  10 %), scaled to 3.3 dB rms, zero mean over any third of an octave so the fitted body keeps its
  shape. Its features are about 8 cents wide (resonances with a Q near 50, in the range measured on
  cellos). It is a table every 2.5 cents (`fine`), from a fixed seed.
- **Measured notes still play as recorded**: the curve is added at every pitch, so each measured
  note's correction holds what the curve doesn't already give at its harmonics (`corr − fine`).
- **It moves energy, not the level**: read every step at the step's pitch, it is normalised over the
  harmonics at once, so it reshapes the spectrum and leaves the level. Its normalisation at first rode
  on the harmonics' amplitudes, which follow over 30 ms: a slur sweeping the harmonics across the
  detail faster than that bumped the level by up to 4 dB. A judgment: a vibrato's swing of the whole
  level (about 2 dB with the detail as it is) goes with it; each harmonic's own swing (1-7 dB at
  ±18 cents) stays.
- **Within a note it comes in with the first glide** after the hand-off, so the bank starts exactly as
  the recording is.

The recordings are 3.8 cents rms off equal temperament (−12 to +9), and with the detail a measured
note played at its equal-tempered pitch meets it a few cents from where its recording did: that, not
the model, is most of what the distance lost with it (below).

### The bow's movement (`tools/life.py`)

A held bowed note isn't still. Over each sustain's steady part `tools/life.py` measures:

| | recordings (median) | renders |
|---|---|---|
| pitch, trend removed | 1.58 cents rms, spread fairly evenly over 0.25-16 Hz | 1.09 (1.20 in Phase 2: another set of random paths; 20 paths of F#3 read 1.36 in both) |
| level, below 2 Hz (per octave from 0.25 Hz) | 0.53 0.39 0.41 dB | 0.52 0.35 0.34 |
| level, 2-16 Hz | 0.27 0.20 0.17 dB | left out (below) |
| each harmonic against the level | 2.38 dB (median over harmonics) | 1.47 (set by the bands: below) |
| ... by harmonic number | 1.3 dB on the fundamental, 1.7 the 2nd, 2.4 the 8th, 3.0 the 20th | 0.8, 0.9, 1.7, 1.6 |
| ... in shared patterns: the strongest one's share, the strongest three's | 36 %, 70 % (independent harmonics: about 10 %, 30 %) | 43 %, 75 % |
| a band's level, 0.25-25 Hz (0.5-2 and 2-8 kHz), recording over render per cell (four paths each) | 1 | median 0.96, 0.77-1.08 (10-90 %) |
| the noise's pulse at the period, 250 Hz-16 kHz | 0.03 0.07 0.11 0.17 0.22 0.37 0.51 | 0.03 0.08 0.07 0.12 0.12 0.30 0.44 |

(Spectra per octave of modulation frequency compare the same way: `life.py` prints them.) The pitch and level
movement varies by dynamic with no clear trend (pitch: mp 0.96 c, f 2.31), so the engine stores it per
note, pooled over the 4 dynamics × 2 bows and smoothed across notes (a median of three).

**The harmonics move together, in patterns.** Their mean correlation is near 0 (0.03), which first
read as independent, but the patterns mix up and down: the strongest principal component of a
recording's harmonic movement holds 36 % of it and the strongest three 70 %, where independent
harmonics would give about 10 % and 30 %. Each performance has its own: the down and up bow of a
note share their strongest pattern only loosely (|cos| 0.37, chance 0.21), and pooled over a note's 8
recordings the strongest three hold 48 %. They aren't a spectral tilt (a line in log k: 6 %); pitch
and level together explain 19 % (a harmonic moves 0.32 dB per cent of pitch: sharper resonances than
the fitted body's 50-cent detail). The measuring floor is small: a still render measures 0.08 c,
0.06 dB and 0.11 dB. So the engine keeps, per note and dynamic, its down-bow recording's (whose onset
it plays) three patterns (a shape over harmonics 1-20 and how fast each moves) and what is left over,
per harmonic, moving independently (above the 20th, as the 20th: a judgment, as the recordings'
higher harmonics are too near the noise to measure; growing with k as at first played a flutter).

The first version moved every harmonic independently at the measured size. The owner heard it as
weird, and Still Bow (no movement) as good: independent random movement on 160 harmonics is the
watery, chorus-like sound additive synthesis is known for.

**Its size is set by what the ear hears: how a band's level moves** (`tools/calibrate_flicker.py`).
Measured harmonic by harmonic in dB, a weak harmonic's reading is jumpy with the noise around it, so
the recordings' bands move much less than their weak harmonics seem to. Played at the measured
size, C3's upper band (2-8 kHz) swelled 2.6× and fluttered 2× as much as its recordings' (the owner
heard fast muting of the highs at 15-20 Hz, with dips about once a second, on low notes only). So
per cell the harmonics' movement takes a gain (`tools/flicker_adjust.json`, 0.05-2×) that brings the
level movement of two bands (0.5-2 and 2-8 kHz, 0.25-25 Hz) to its two recordings'. The per-harmonic
sizes in the table then read lower than the recordings', as they should.

Each cell is rendered on four random paths and their movement averaged: one path's slow movement
varies by about 25 % from path to path, and with one render a cell the calibration chased that (in
Phase 3 four cells went to the floor of 0.2 on one path each, where ten paths showed them moving as
the old engine's had). Recalibrated so for the fine detail (whose share of the movement comes with the
pitch's wander): two passes, then within 0.77-1.08 of the recordings (10-90 %, median 0.96). The cells still furthest off (C3 f, C2 mf, C5 f, F#2 f) move more
than their recordings even with no flicker at all: their level's own movement.

The engine plays each as band-limited noise, one RBJ band-pass per octave (0.25-32 Hz, Q √2) scaled
to its measured rms, on the voice's 32-sample step:

- **Pitch** (cents) and **level** (dB, shared with the noise) as sums of octave bands. **The level
  moves only below 2 Hz** (`kLevelOcts`, then a low-pass at 3 Hz): a judgment. The recordings' level
  also moves 0.2-0.3 dB per octave at 2-16 Hz, but played alone, not tied to the rest of what the bow
  does, the owner heard it as fast, random volume fluctuations.
- **The harmonics' patterns**: each a score (a sum of octave bands, as the pitch, up to 16 Hz and
  closed above 20 Hz as the leftover movement is) times its shape. Without that top, the patterns'
  measured 16-64 Hz octaves put sidebands between the harmonics: C3 had 14-25 dB more there than its
  recording (heard as noise in the sustain).
- **Each harmonic's leftover movement**: three two-octave bands (0.25-16 Hz), closed above 20 Hz by a
  second-order low-pass, updated every 4th step (345 Hz at 44.1 kHz). Both are measured against the
  note's level, so they move energy between the harmonics and leave the level alone: each step takes
  out what they would add in total.
- Each harmonic's rotator carries its amplitude, and each step aims it at its new level by a growth
  per sample: a smooth ramp at no cost per sample.
- The filters start in their steady state (a draw of their state's covariance), so the slowest
  movement (1-2 s to build up from rest) is there from the first moment.

Lessons:

- **Calibrate it** (`tools/calibrate_life.py`, the piano's calibrate_noise.py's way): `life.py`'s
  slowest octaves read low (a 3 s stretch, trend removed, a 2.6 s window). The data is built from
  those readings and the renders are read the same way, so the bias counts twice: they measured at
  half. Three passes of a per-octave correction brought them level (`tools/life_adjust.json`; the
  harmonics' part was restarted for the patterns).
- **Check the structure, not only the size.** Matching each harmonic's rms and spectrum, and a mean
  correlation near 0, still let the first version sound wrong: the principal components show it.
- **Judge movement by bands, not harmonics.** Per-harmonic dB readings overstate weak harmonics'
  movement; a band's level is what the ear follows (`calibrate_flicker.py`). And check the spectrum
  between the harmonics with the movement on: modulation that is too fast shows there as noise.
- **A harmonic's movement needs a steep top.** A band-pass's 6 dB/octave skirt left sidebands 20-30 dB
  under each harmonic, 30-130 Hz from it: over the bow noise between the harmonics, hiding its pulse
  (0.04 measured against 0.2-0.4) and roughening the tone. So does a gain changed in steps (32
  samples), which is why the gains ramp.
- **Not by the distance.** A random path can't follow the recording's own, so the perceptual
  distance can't judge the movement: two renders of the same note (two random paths) were 2.8 % apart
  with the first version's movement (median, 1.7-4.9; now 1.2-1.3), and the distance rose from 3.0 to 4.3 % with the first version's movement on
  while its signed differences (which way the sound is wrong) stayed within 1 %. The statistics and
  the owner's ears are the measure.

### The bow noise

The residual (the recording less its harmonics) over the steady part, as power in nine octave bands
(62.5 Hz-16 kHz), relative to the harmonic level. The engine plays unit-variance white noise through
two RBJ band-pass stages per band (Q 1.2), **each band from its own noise**: from one, neighbouring
bands' outputs are correlated and partly cancel where they overlap (they came out 6-13 dB short).
Their powers then add, and `make_header.py` solves the bands' gains against the filters' responses.
The noise comes in with the model during the hand-off (the onset's own noise is in the recording) and
follows the dynamic and the level's movement.

**Calibrated** (`tools/calibrate_noise.py`, as the piano's): the analysis reads the noise as the
residual, and its harmonics (heterodyned over 3 periods) take in the noise within about a third of
the harmonic spacing: on low notes, most of it. So the stored levels read low (7-8 dB at C2, nothing
from F#5 up) and the engine, playing them faithfully, measured low by that again. Three passes of a
correction per cell and band (`tools/noise_adjust.json`) bring the renders' residual to the
recordings' within 0.5 dB (median), and, checked another way (the spectrum between the harmonics,
±f0/4 notched, 34 cells), within ±1.2 dB per band (median).

**The pulse** (`grainDepth`, `grainPhase`). In the recordings the bow noise comes in bursts, once per
vibration of the string, as the bow catches and slips: its envelope's component at the fundamental's
phase, per band, is 0.2-0.5 of its mean above 2 kHz (by chance: 0.01). Where in the period it peaks is
a property of the bow's direction: down-bow and up-bow are 171° apart (median), as reversing the bow
mirrors the string's motion. The engine shapes each band's noise by u^p (u = (1 + cos(θ − ψ)) / 2,
θ the fundamental's phase from its rotator), with p for the measured depth (`grainExp`) and the
down-bow's ψ (the onsets are down-bow), normalised to keep the band's power. Grain scales the depth.

### The release

The sustains are cut mid-bow for looping, so the only release in the library is the staccatos', which
end in the string's own decay. Per staccato, each harmonic's level is fitted as a line from where the
note has fallen 6 dB from its peak to 36 dB; harmonics are pooled by octave band, then over every
dynamic and round robin of the note (15-16 staccatos), then smoothed across neighbouring notes (a
median of three per band, in log: one note's set scatters, C5's fundamental 163 dB/s against A4's 31).
Result: 30-60 dB/s low in the range, rising to 150-200 dB/s at the top and in the highest bands.

A key let go during the onset: the recording fades at 80 dB/s (`kOnsetReleaseDbS`, a judgment) and
still hands over, the harmonics starting at the level it has decayed to, so a short note rings down
as a string does.

## Playing (Phase 3a)

None of this is in the recordings (no vibrato, no slurs, one dynamic per take), so each is a
judgment built on what they do measure; where a number comes from elsewhere it says where.

- **Continuous dynamics.** The dynamic (p 0 … f 3) sets the level, the source spectrum (brighter
  when louder, as measured), the corrections' blend, the noise and its pulse: everything the four
  layers measure. *Dynamics From: Velocity* keeps it for the note. *Controller*: the velocity picks
  the onset (how hard the bow catches, played at that layer's level), and the Dynamics control
  (0-100 %, CC 1 and CC 11, smoothed over 30 ms) sets the sustain: after the hand-off the harmonics
  move from the onset's levels to the controller's dynamic, and follow it as it moves (re-placed past
  1 % of a layer; the harmonics follow over 30 ms, `kFollowS`). A soft attack can swell, an accent can
  fall back (sfp).
- **Vibrato** (Vibrato depth, Rate, Delay, Attack, Vary). Cellists measure about 5.3-6.5 Hz and 10-17.5
  cents either side of the note, wider in cents higher up a string (arXiv 2512.18162: TinySOL and
  concerto recordings of Fournier, Rostropovich, Ma; string players generally 0.2-0.35 semitone).
  So the depth given is for a note a fifth above its open string and scales with the string's length
  there (2^(semitones above the open string / 12), held between the open string and two and a half
  octaves up): the same movement of the hand. A sine, from its delay brought in over its attack
  (smoothstep), its rate and depth wandering slowly (one-pole at 1 Hz: Vary 100 % is ±12 % rate and
  ±50 % depth, rms). It moves the harmonics through the body's fine detail (above): 1-7 dB per harmonic
  at ±18 cents. The onset recording follows the pitch too (a vibrato, bend or slur during it). It
  starts again with each bow, and carries on through a slur.
- **Legato** (the kit's Legato voice mode): a key pressed while another is held slurs: no new onset
  or bow, the pitch glides over Glide (40 ms by default: a finger change; longer is a portamento).
  Everything that depends on where the pitch is (level, corrections, release, the movement's size,
  the noise) follows it as it moves; a slur down adds harmonics (silent, rising to their levels), a
  slur up silences those above 16 kHz and anything that would fold over. *Mono* bows each note again.
- **A string bowed again** (Mono, a re-bowed string, a voice taken): what it played fades over 10 ms
  (`kRebowS`), then the note starts afresh.
- **One Note Each** (Strings, in Poly): four strings, one note each, as on the cello. A key goes to
  the highest string tuned below it (an open string's note to the next string down, which is how a
  player gives it vibrato; below C2 the C string): the voice slot is the string. If that string is
  held, the next one down; if every string that can play it is held, the highest slurs to it (the left
  hand moves while the bow goes on, over Glide). A ringing string is bowed again, cutting its ring.
  The vibrato's depth follows the string the note is on (in Free, the string it would be on).
- **A slur's articulation** (Slur, 0-100 %, default 50; the owner: "a new note often forces a pause
  as the finger moves", and the vibrato starts again). The tone dips as the finger lifts and lands
  (raised-cosine ramps: down 15 ms, held 50 ms × Slur, back over 35-70 ms; 12 dB × Slur deep, so 6 dB
  at 50 %), the bow noise doesn't (the bow goes on). The vibrato's depth falls to nothing over 25 ms,
  its cycle starts again from the note's centre once it has faded, and it comes back sooner than on a
  new bow (a quarter of Delay, 0.4 of Attack: 75 ms and 200 ms at the defaults), so short slurred notes
  have it too. A judgment: the recordings have no slurs. What a player does ahead of the change (the
  anticipation) can't be known in time without looking ahead.
- **Even Notes** (0-100 %, default 100). The recorded notes were played a few dB louder or softer
  each: per dynamic, about 3 dB rms off a quadratic along the keyboard (A3 4-7 dB over it at every
  dynamic, A4 4-10 under, F#3 3-5 under). Some of that may be the cello (some notes radiate more), but
  in a phrase it was heard as uneven, and a player keeps a line even. At 100 % a note's level is the
  curve's (`levelEven`), its onset scaled with it; each keeps its own timbre. The largest level step
  between neighbouring semitones (mf) is then 0.9 dB, from 3.2. A judgment.
- **Bow Weight** (−100…+100 %, default 0; the owner found the tone edgy and bright, and asked for a way
  to soften it without cutting the top off). The renders match the recordings' spectral balance
  (within 0.5 dB to 5 kHz; 0.7-1.4 dB brighter above, as in Phase 2): the edge is the recordings'
  (close-miked, much of their energy at 1-2.5 kHz). In bowed strings the brightness follows the bow's
  force, the level mostly its speed, and the recordings' dynamics measure the spectrum of each: from p
  to f the harmonics grow against the fundamental by 3 dB at the 5th, 8 at the 12th, 13 from the 20th.
  So Bow Weight plays the source spectrum (and corrections) of a lighter or heavier bow along that axis
  (±100 % = ±1.5 layers, extrapolated at most half a layer past p and f) at the level the dynamic
  plays: a gradual tilt, not a cut. At −100 % the 5-10 kHz band falls 4-8 dB, the level not at all.
  The 1-2.5 kHz edge changes less (the layers differ little there); a room (Phase 3d) is the other
  half of what softens a close-miked string.
- **What a slur doesn't have**: the left finger's own sound and the bow's small change as the string
  shortens (not measurable here). The kit's legato glides at a constant time whatever the interval.

## The room (Phase 3d)

The recordings are dry and close-miked; a listener hears a cello in a room, and the room is much of
what takes the edge off a close-miked string. The room is synthkit's `dsp::Reverb` (API.md §8b),
10d6/piano's Space reverb moved into the kit for this (its docs/MODEL.md, Space, has the design and
measurements): each space's early reflections are an image-source impulse played by a convolver, its
tail a 16-line feedback delay network. Room, Chamber, Hall (default), Cathedral and Plate, and the
piano's controls with the same IDs and ranges (Space panel).

- **After the voices**, in `processGlobal`: the voices are mono, so the dry sound stays as it is and
  the space's wet signal is added in stereo (its left and right decorrelated: correlation about 0).
- **Its level** is twice the piano's (`kWet`): a held note masks its own room, where a piano's decay
  lets it through (at the piano's level, Reverb 20 % put the room 19 dB under a held note). At the
  default, Hall at 25 %, the room is 11 dB under a held mf note (−20 dB on the legato phrase at 20 %
  before doubling); at 100 % about as loud as it. A judgment, by those numbers; Dry (Close Mic) and
  Distant Hall are presets for either end.
- **Everything compared with a recording renders dry** (`common.DRY`: `space.room` 0 in measure.py,
  handoff.py, life.py and the calibrations), so none of the measurements move.
- **Starting.** The kit's reverb prepares every space when the engine starts (37 ms natively, once its
  tail energies were measured to 0.6 × RT60 and the rest summed: 90 ms at the piano's 1.5 × RT60). In
  the browser that, on top of every voice working out the same wander-filter statistics, put the first
  notes 1 s after a click; the synth now works those out once per sample rate and the voices copy them
  (start-up 275 → 59 ms natively), and the first notes play within 0.5 s again.
- **Cost** in the browser: about 15 % of a core (four low notes at f: 2.9× realtime with the room, 5.3×
  dry); natively about 2.6 % of a core. At Reverb 0 it stops once it has rung out and costs nothing.

## Sympathetic strings (Phase 3d)

The open strings a note isn't played on ring along with it: the piano's free-string bank (its
docs/MODEL.md, Sympathetic resonance), for four strings. Each open string's partials up to 2 kHz
(72 resonators) are narrow complex resonators at the string's pitch, decaying at its measured release
rate (`releaseDbS` at its note, by octave band), driven by everything the cello plays (the dry sound,
before the room) through the bridge: z += K·x/fs. The strings are tuned in pure fifths down from A3
(as cellists tune: C2 6 cents, G2 4, D3 2 under equal temperament), so a coinciding harmonic beats a
little against its string. A string a note is played on is stopped by the finger (each voice reports
its string every block, `stopMask`): its open modes die in a third of a second and aren't driven. A
note on the open string's own pitch is played on the next string down (vibrato, above), so a G2 sets
the open G ringing.

**What the recordings show** (`tools/sympathy.py`, the staccatos' releases): 0.3 s after a staccato has
fallen 36 dB, its harmonics on an open string's partial (within 15 cents) are at −79.5 dB against its
peak, the others at −92.9 (the noise floor): free strings ringing on, 13 dB over the floor. In the
release itself those harmonics decay more slowly at 125-250 Hz (37 against 48 dB/s), not above 250 Hz.

**The coupling is a judgment** (`kSympK`, scaled by Resonance, −40…+24 dB). Matching that late ring
(`tools/calibrate_sympathy.py`: the ring alone, the difference of renders with and without it, read at
the same harmonics and moment) took K = 69/s, at which a held note rings the free strings as loud as
itself (G3 on the open G 16 dB louder): implausible. A bank driven by the sound can't give both; the
recordings' late ring is likely the note's own string and its open neighbour coupled, decaying
together and slower, which this doesn't model. A jolt to every free string at each bow's attack, sized
to that ring, was tried and put out peaks of +25 dB (the ring sits only near the note's harmonics,
not at every open partial). And the recordings already hold the cello's own sympathetic ring in their
measured spectra: at K = 2.2 the bank moved the distance from them (4.2 → 4.5 %). So K = 0.55/s: a note
with no unison rings the strings 42 dB under itself while held, G3 on the open G 25 dB under; 0.5 s
after its release G3's ring is 13 dB under its own tail. The distance doesn't move. Resonance +12 dB
(*Ringing Strings*) is a clear halo; the bank costs about 8 % of the browser's time.

## Body Size and the wolf (Phase 3d)

- **Body Size** (50-200 %): a body larger by λ has its resonances lower by λ, so the body (its fit,
  the measured notes' corrections and its fine detail) is read at f·λ; the string and its source
  spectrum stay, and the level. Smaller moves the resonances up, towards a viola's or violin's body: at
  50 % C3's fundamental is under the body's lowest resonance and nearly gone (23 dB less under 250 Hz),
  as on a small instrument. Larger moves them down, towards a bass's. A judgment: a real body differs
  in more than its size. Presets *Small Body* (75 %) and *Big Cello* (135 %).
- **Wolf** (0-100 %, off by default; the recorded cello has none) and **Wolf Note** (130-260 Hz,
  default 175, about F3; most cellos' wolves are between E3 and F#3). Where the body's main resonance
  couples with the string's fundamental, the note can't settle. Near the wolf note (a Gaussian 35 cents
  wide in the note's pitch) the tone dips by up to 14 dB × Wolf, about 7 times a second (faster the
  nearer), its rate wandering as the vibrato's does: at 100 % on F3 the level swings 14 dB; five
  semitones away, nothing. A judgment. Preset *Old Cello With a Wolf* (60 %).

## Bow colour: position and the mute (Phase 3c)

Neither is in the recordings (no sul tasto, ponticello or mute), so both are judgments, built on
bowed-string and mute acoustics and, where they can be, on the recordings' own dynamics. Bow
pressure is Bow Weight (Phase 3a).

- **Bow Position** (−100 % sul tasto … 0 … +100 % sul ponticello). The bowing point β, as a share of
  the string from the bridge, goes from 1/4 through 1/10 (normal) to 1/40; players use about 1/8 to
  1/32 on the violin. Moving towards the bridge, players slow the bow and press harder, which is most
  of the brilliance there (Guettler), and bow force brightens the tone as bow speed darkens it. So:
  - the **source** moves along the recordings' own dynamics, a layer per 100 % (brighter towards the
    bridge, darker towards the fingerboard). The source alone: the measured notes' corrections carry
    each take's quirks (moving them too, sul tasto took 12.5 dB off C3's fundamental: its mp take's);
  - the partials with a **node at the bowing point** are weakened, to 0.3 at the node (the bow hair's
    width fills the rest), the more the further from normal;
  - past what the dynamics reach, a **tilt**: near the bridge the fundamental 6 dB weaker and the
    partials above the 4th +6 dB per octave (glassy, whistling); over the fingerboard −5 dB per octave
    above the 3rd (soft);
  - the **bow noise** grows at both ends (+8 dB at the bridge with a harder pulse, scratchy; +6 dB over
    the fingerboard with less of it, airy: flautando).
  On C3 at mf: spectral centroid 443 Hz normal, 318 at −100 %, 902 at +50 %, 2067 at +100 %; the
  level stays. Presets *Sul Tasto* (−70 %) and *Sul Ponticello* (+70 %).
- **Mute** (0-100 %). Measured on violins (Acta Acustica 2025): every mute dips the bridge's
  resonance region ("bridge hill"), a heavy practice mute deeper and wider, and heavy mutes take the
  lows and much of the loudness too. Here: a dip around 1.8 kHz (0.8 octave wide, up to 12 dB), a
  gentler cut above 600 Hz (up to 6 dB), and past 50 % up to 6 dB off everything; on the bow noise too.
  It isn't normalised back to the level. 50 % is about a performance mute (C3: −0.3 dB, 1-6 kHz about
  −7 dB), 100 % a heavy practice mute (−6 dB, 1-6 kHz −19 dB). Presets *Con Sordino* (50 %) and
  *Practice Mute*.

## Pizzicato (Phase 3b)

The **Articulation** choice (`play.articulation`: Arco, Pizzicato) or a keyswitch (C1 Arco, F1
Pizzicato: an octave under the cello's range, swallowed in `processEvents`, which moves the choice with
`ctx.setParam`, so the page and the host follow) sets how the next notes are played. Each note keeps
the articulation chosen when it arrives (`processEvents` records it per key; the voice reads it in
`start`, where parameters can't be read): a change during a note affects the next. D1, E1, G1 and A1
are Staccato, Spiccato, Tremolo and Harmonics (below). The choices are appended as they come, so
patches keep their indices: Arco, Pizzicato, Staccato, Spiccato, Tremolo, Harmonics.

A plucked note is a free decay: after the pluck it is the string's harmonics ringing down. It is
measured from bigcat's pizzicato (`tools/pizz.py` → `tools/pizz.json` + `tools/pizz_onsets.npz` →
`tools/make_pizz_header.py` → `src/PizzData.h`): 13 notes, C2-C5 a minor third apart, × 4 round robins
(0.75-7 s, each recorded until it has rung into the noise).

```
python3 -I tools/pizz.py ../bigcat tools/pizz.json tools/pizz_onsets.npz     # ~10 min on two cores
python3 -I tools/make_pizz_header.py tools/pizz.json tools/pizz_onsets.npz src/PizzData.h
python3 -I tools/measure_pizz.py ../bigcat build/strings_render              # ~2 min
```

- **The pluck as recorded**: each take's first 0.22 s, transposed to the note's pitch (the nearest
  measured note: 1.5 semitones at most from C2 to C5), crossfading at 0.15-0.21 s into the modes.
  Handed over at 0.06-0.10 s at first, the modes were 2-7 dB under their recordings until 0.15 s on mid
  and high notes: the pluck's own ring (the body, the finger) is in the recording for that long.
- **The modes** (the piano's, its docs/MODEL.md: Modes). A plucked string's harmonic is a few decaying
  sinusoids a little apart in frequency (its two polarisations, its coupling to the body), so it beats
  and decays in two stages. Fitted to each harmonic's envelope (heterodyned, from the hand-off), one
  mode leaves 3.1 dB of error (median over the takes, dB against the envelope), two 2.1 (3 modes, tried
  on a few notes: a little better again). A second mode is kept where it lowers the error by 15 % and
  passes the guards (no near-cancelling pairs; decays 3-400 dB/s; offsets within ±6 Hz): 1220 modes in
  all, at most 86 in a take, to 10 kHz. Weighted by the note's level^−0.9 (each moment about as it is
  heard; weighted by the harmonic's own level, its noisy tail counted as much as its start).
- **Where each harmonic is.** A free string is a little stiff: its partials sit off the harmonic series
  (C2's 8th-12th 13-17 cents sharp). Each harmonic is moved to its own peak (outliers against its
  neighbours replaced by their median); a stiff-string line through them fitted worse.
- **The recording bounds each mode.** Every take rings into the noise, so no mode may stand more than
  6 dB over its harmonic's envelope (or floor) at any time: one that would decays as fast as that needs
  (135 were held). At first weak harmonics' tails were fitted as modes that never decayed,
  and D#3 rang on above 1 kHz 15-30 dB over its recording.
- **What reads as a pitch glide isn't one.** The pitch seen in the first 0.2 s swings up and down (C2
  +22, −14, +14 cents at 40, 60, 80 ms) the same way in every round robin: modes beating, carried by
  their frequency offsets. There is no tension glide to add.
- **Between the measured notes**: the nearest note's take, its modes moved to the pitch (frequencies
  and offsets by the ratio) and decaying at their rates scaled to how fast a note there falls (each
  note's first second, interpolated in log; above C5 along the top interval, at most twice C5's). The
  body follows the pitch as it does for the bowed notes: the fitted body read where each harmonic is now
  against where it was recorded, and the fine detail, normalised so the level stays. So vibrato and
  bends move the harmonics through the body, Body and Body Size change it, and a measured note at its
  recorded pitch plays as recorded.
- **Velocity**: the level by v², as the library's own sfz maps it (no velocity layers: the round robins
  span no dynamic, within about 3 dB); velocity 127 plays a take at its recorded level, about as loud as
  a bowed f over its first 0.3 s, and velocity 30 25 dB under it. A softer pluck is also darker (a
  judgment): a first-order high shelf above 1.2 kHz, down by 12 dB × (1 − v), on the pluck and the
  modes alike.
- **Even Notes** applies as to the bowed notes: the plucked notes were played about as unevenly (2.6 dB rms off a quadratic
  along the keys: A2 5 dB over, A3 4 over, C4 and D#4 4 under).
- **Letting go** lifts the finger, which damps the string: 60 dB/s on top of each mode's own decay (a
  judgment), scaled by Release; the sustain pedal holds it off, so the note rings as measured. Held, a
  note rings down by itself and its voice ends.
- **Round robins**: each key cycles its four takes in order.
- **The rest**: vibrato (as the bowed notes': the left hand can rock a plucked note), the mute, the
  sympathetic strings and the room apply. The bow's controls (the Bow panel, Bow Noise, Grain, Bow
  Weight, Bow Position, Dynamics, Slur, the wolf) don't; the page greys them while Pizzicato is chosen.
  A plucked note is always plucked again, in every voice mode (Legato doesn't slur it; the kit's glide
  still moves the pitch).

**How close it is** (`tools/measure_pizz.py`: every take, played on its key at velocity 127, Even
Notes off, dry, against its recording over 0-3 s): **8.0 %** by the perceptual distance (attack 0.0,
early 5.8, sustain 17.2), against **16.8 %** for the same note's next round robin (another pluck). The
hand-off: the 10 ms level over 0.04-0.2 s within 1.0 dB of the recording (median; worst 2.7). The
decay: 0.5 s and 1 s on, −0.2 and −1.5 dB against the recording (median). The cells furthest off are
D#3 (17-23 %) and C5 (16-30 %), which are as far from their own other takes (12-44 % and 22-90 %): their
recordings hold the open strings ringing along (D#3 and C5 with the open G at 98 Hz, the C string's 2nd
at 130 Hz) more than the sympathetic bank gives.

Browser speed: a four-string chord at f struck 16 times with the pedal down (every voice ringing)
2.5× realtime.

A plucked note far from the pitch it was plucked at (a wide bend with a deep vibrato and Pitch Wander
up, or Mono's voice moving to another note) turns each mode exactly: the series that serves vibrato
grew there, and the check's random patches found a note at +718 dB (PR #12 fixed it).

## Strokes: staccato and spiccato (Phase 3b)

bigcat's staccatos are whole short strokes: the bow bites, the stroke peaks at 0.12-0.31 s, the bow
leaves (the level 6 dB under its peak) at 0.14-0.66 s (median 0.28), the string rings down; the files
end 0.5-1.1 s in, some mid-ring. A staccato is a bowed note played from its own take (`tools/staccato.py`
→ `tools/stacc.json` + `tools/stacc_onsets.npz` → `tools/make_stacc_header.py` → `src/StaccData.h`):

```
python3 -I tools/staccato.py ../bigcat tools/stacc.json tools/stacc_onsets.npz    # ~1 min
python3 -I tools/make_stacc_header.py tools/stacc.json tools/stacc_onsets.npz src/StaccData.h
python3 -I tools/measure_stacc.py ../bigcat build/strings_render                  # ~1 min
```

- **Its take**: the nearest measured note's, at the velocity's nearest dynamic, two round robins per
  note and dynamic (the two closest to the four's median envelope; the others are the yardstick).
  Repeated staccatos are what the articulation is for, and one take would repeat exactly.
- **Recorded until 0.25 s at most**: the take hands over at its bow-off or 0.25 s, whichever is first,
  crossfading over 50 ms into the bank started in phase with it (the arco notes' machinery: each
  harmonic's level and phase at the crossfade's middle, measured on the take). Recording every stroke
  to its bow-off would take 2.7 MB a round robin; as it is, the onsets are 3.7 MB of samples (the page
  grew from 7.1 to 12.4 MB).
- **The bow leaves when the take's did**, whatever the key does: a key let go earlier changes nothing,
  a key held on changes nothing. A stroke longer than its recording fades as its take did from the
  hand-off to the bow-off (dB/s, measured): gliding to a held note's level instead, the bow left 200-345
  ms late on F#2 and A2.
- **The release is the take's own**, each harmonic as it decayed (from bow-off until 30 dB down, its
  floor or 0.5 s; weak ones and failed fits take their neighbours' median), where that's faster than
  the notes' pooled release (on the pooled one, the tails were 6.6 dB too bright at 1-2 kHz 0.15-0.45 s
  after the bow left), never slower (a low fundamental's half-second fit, beating and ringing along,
  read 16-19 dB/s on some takes, and stress found C3's ringing 3 s on at 10 dB/s).
- **The level**: the staccatos' stroke level (their harmonics' peak, the round robins' median) at the
  velocity, between notes and dynamics as the arco levels are, with its own Even Notes curve.

**How close it is** (`tools/measure_stacc.py`: each kept take on its key at its dynamic's velocity, Even
Notes off, dry, against its recording for as long as the file runs, at most 1 s): **23.5 %** by the
perceptual distance (attack 0.1, early 9.6, late 57.0), against **26.1 %** for the same note and
dynamic's other takes. Bow-off within 2 ms of the recording (median), the peak within 0.6 dB. The late
window (0.5-1 s, the ring after the bow) is the largest part: the recordings hold the body's and the
open strings' ring under high notes (−16 dB under 250 Hz 0.15-0.45 s after the bow), which the bank
doesn't give (as for the arco release: a known gap).

**Spiccato** (a judgment: bigcat has none) is the same takes bounced: the bow leaves just after the
stroke has reached its level, 40 ms after it stands 10 dB under its peak (50-120 ms in), so the
recording hands over there (30 ms crossfade, the harmonics measured there too) and the string rings
down at once. The take is one dynamic lighter, and its level at the bounce 3 dB under the staccato's
stroke at the same velocity. Not measured against anything; judged by eye: separate notes with clear
dips between the bounces at 7 notes a second.

## Tremolo (Phase 3b)

A judgment (no tremolo in these recordings): a held bowed note whose bow changes direction Tremolo Rate
times a second (4-24, default 12), each stroke 15 % longer or shorter at random. At each change the
level dips (a raised cosine to the 4th power, 10 dB deep, about a fifth of a stroke wide), the bow
noise bursts (9 dB more at the change) and each stroke's level varies by 1 dB rms. It runs from the
note's start, the recorded onset included (the first stroke starts mid-way: no dip at the attack).
Notes started in the same block share their strokes (the tremolo's random draws are seeded by the
block), so a double stop is one bow: on separate clocks a fifth's dips blurred. Arco's slurs, vibrato,
Dynamics and the rest apply.

## Harmonics (Phase 3b)

Measured from bigcat's four harmonic slides (`tools/touch.py` → `src/TouchData.h`): each bows an open
string (C2, G2, D3, A3) for half a second, then a finger brushes along it across its harmonic nodes. At
a node 1/n along, the touched string keeps its harmonics that are multiples of n: the note sounds n
times the open pitch, its k-th harmonic the string's n·k-th. On the C and G strings the frequencies stay
on the open string's harmonics; on D and A the finger also partly stops the string between nodes
(partials sweep in arcs), so only clear node frames are used (the first three multiples of n 18 dB
over the mean of the others: 0.1-1.8 s of frames per string and node, n = 2-6).

- **What the finger takes**: per node and string, the kept harmonics' shape against the open string's
  own bowed spectrum at the same harmonics, pooled by m (the median: one string's node scatters ±10 dB):
  0 at m = 1-2, −3 dB at the 3rd, −7.5 at the 4th, about −9 to −10 from the 6th on. A purer, flute-like
  tone.
- **The level**: 7 dB under the open string bowed alike (the median over 19 nodes × strings; −3 to −9
  by node, the A string's loud open start reads −22).
- **Which harmonic**: a natural harmonic of an open string where one sounds at the key (nodes 2-6, within
  half a semitone: C3, G3, C4, E4, G4 on the C string, and so on), else an artificial one on the lowest
  string it fits (a stopped note touched a fourth above, node 4; else a fifth, 3; else an octave, 2).
  Below C3 none fits and the note is bowed as usual. The pitch is the key's (natural harmonics' own
  pitches are 14 cents flat on node 5: not played).
- **The sound**: the bowed source read at the string's n·k-th harmonics (Bow Weight, Bow Position as
  for any bowed note), less what the finger takes, through the body at k times the pitch as always; the
  measured notes' corrections are left out (they belong to stopped notes). No recording starts it: it
  swells in over 80 ms (a judgment) at its steady spectrum, then lives, moves and releases as a bowed
  note. 4-7 dB under a bowed note at the same velocity.

## How close it is (`tools/measure.py`)

Every measured note and dynamic rendered as recorded (the layer's middle velocity, held, 44.1 kHz,
Even Notes off so each plays at its recording's level),
against its down-bow recording over 0-3 s by the perceptual distance (`tools/perceptual.py`, the
piano's): median **4.2 %** (attack 0, early 1.8, sustain 10.5). The attack is the recording itself,
so 0 there is by construction (4 cells now play another take's onset: 3.9 % before).

- **The timbre alone** (the recording's level movement applied to the render after the hand-off):
  **3.0 %** (sustain 7.6). Both were 3.0 and 2.3 % with the note held still (Phase 1), 4.3 and 3.3 %
  with every harmonic moving independently, and 3.8 and 2.8 % before the body's fine detail.
- **At the recordings' own pitch** (`--at-recorded-pitch`: A4 moved per note so it plays where its
  recording does; they're 3.8 cents rms off equal temperament): **3.5 %**, timbre **2.7 %** (sustain
  6.6), as before the fine detail (3.5 and 2.7). With the detail, a measured note played a few cents
  from its recording meets the body somewhere else, as a cello's would; that is what the distance
  lost, not the model.
- **The yardstick**: the same note's up-bow recording against the down-bow, 20.1 %: another
  performance (its own envelope and onset).
- **Even along the keyboard** (every semitone C2-C6 at mf, as recorded): the largest level step
  between neighbouring semitones is 3.2 dB (median 0.9), against 7.9 dB between measured notes a
  minor third apart (median 2.2); with Even Notes (the default) 0.9 dB (median 0.3) and 1.4 (0.4). Neighbouring semitones' brightness differs a little more than before the
  fine detail (median 0.53 octave of spectral centroid, from 0.45): each note meets the body's
  resonances its own way, as on a cello.
- **The hand-off** (`tools/handoff.py`): the level 0.27-0.44 s dips 2.4 dB below the level around
  it (median), where the recordings themselves dip 1.9 dB at the same moment (the bow's own
  movement): the render dips 0.2 dB more than its own recording (median), 3.4 dB at worst. The
  movement starts after the crossfade; starting during it made the hand-off worse (0.6 dB).

## Speed

In the browser (Chromium; `synthkit/web/test/bench-web.cjs`): four low notes at f (C2, G2, D3, A3:
the most harmonics) 2.6× realtime with the room and the sympathetic strings (4.9× dry, over the
scenario's 9 s; 2.9× and 5.3× before the strings; 3.3× over its first 6 s in Phase 3a, Phase 2:
3.5×, Phase 1: 6.8×), the test chord 3.8×; native (Release) 23× on the four strings. The body's fine detail costs a
table read and an exp per harmonic per step (it and the vibrato: about 6 %). Low
notes cost most (160 harmonics); about 60 % of the time is the harmonic bank's rotators, the rest the
movement's step and the noise (nine bands, each its own random stream). Lessons: the browser's expf/logf are
software routines, so per-harmonic gains use a polynomial `fastExp`/`fastLog2`; a normal draw costs a
log, a cosine and a root, so the movement's noise is uniform (its narrow filters make it Gaussian);
WebAssembly SIMD made no difference (the harmonic sum doesn't vectorise without fast-math), nor
did four partial sums (slower). The
workspace's default build isn't optimised (synthkit forces Release only as the top-level project):
time with `-DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release`.

## What isn't modelled (yet)

- Spiccato and tremolo are judgments (no recordings of either); the harmonics' onset is a swell (the
  slides have none); the seagull effect between harmonic nodes (in the D and A slides) isn't played;
  col legno (the 17 knocks) isn't used.
- Pizzicato above C5 transposes C5's pluck (C6 plays it an octave up, twice as fast); a pluck position
  (the bow position's comb for the finger) isn't modelled; the recordings' own open strings ring more
  than the sympathetic bank gives a plucked note.
- Bow position and the mute measured on this cello (they're judgments: above).
- The late ring of the strings coupled to a note's own (sympathetic strings, above: the bank is driven
  by the sound only).
- The cello's own fine resonances (the engine's fine detail has their statistics, not their shape),
  and a vibrato's swing of the whole level (the detail moves energy between harmonics only).
- A slur's own sound (the finger, the bow's change as the string shortens); the kit's glide is a
  constant time whatever the interval.
- The movement per dynamic (pooled), and its course through a long note (one steady process).
- The up-bow recordings (only the down-bow onsets are kept: alternating bows is a later choice).
- Below C2 and above C6 the nearest note's onset is transposed further; nothing stops playing there.
